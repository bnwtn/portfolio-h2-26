Product
Visualisation

Overview

For my first two years at Checkout.com, the brand relied on stylized illustrations.

In enterprise fintech, payments equal trust, and abstract illustrations weren't giving billion-dollar merchants confidence in the actual platform.

Lead with the
real experience
Genuine product screens and flows, so the value is tangible and credible.
Reveal the
value sequence
Motion and layout show how the product delivers value, step by step.
Balance Clarity
with Expression
Clean, readable and scalable, but still contemporary and engaging.
Flexibility
and Focus
Flexes from stripped-back to expressive depending on message, surface and audience.

I audited 4,000 product screens to establish a repeatable "value sequence": lead with the real product interface to establish immediate clarity, then resolve into a focused, disruptive visual moment.

I documented and shared the system across the team, upskilling colleagues and standardizing how Checkout.com visualizes product value globally.

/ How it works

Three steps along one scale.
Full → Focused → Stylised

Our product visual language is grounded in our native experience.

We start by revealing the interface, using them as the foundation of the story.

From there, we isolate and recompose key propositional elements to guide attention.

Each sequence resolves in a bold, expressive configuration that feels disruptive, yet undeniably real.

Internal
Apps

Proposal
Studio

I built the tool to automate the conversion of completed RFP/RFI spreadsheets (.xlsx) into polished, client-facing Checkout.com proposals.

Reads the bid team's spreadsheet. You drop in an answered RFP or RFI workbook (.xlsx). It sorts the content into headings, paragraphs, bullets, lists, numbered steps and tables, using the 9 patterns defined from 23 real bid workbooks. Unanswered questions are flagged as gaps rather than silently dropped. You review what was found before anything is created.

Lays it out on brand automatically. Content flows into a fixed Checkout design system: A4 pages, consistent margins, headings, page numbers and running section titles. Long sections split across pages on their own. Questions are marked in the prospect's brand colour.

Personalises it to the prospect. It adds the partner name and logo, a choice of cover style (dark hero, or the "WHERE / Partner / CHECKS / OUT" pill cover), a contents page and a matching back cover.

Exports the finished document. It produces a branded PDF at a resolution you choose, a print version with selectable text, or a JSON file you can reopen and keep editing.

At Thrive: Venice, Abu Dhabi and Commercial Kick-Off, every interactive experience was a hand-wired Figma prototype.

Every screen was drawn by hand, and every state was a separate frame. A country, a payment method or an "expanded card" each needed its own artboard. Every click was a noodle dragged by hand.

Change one screen and you recheck every connection. Figma can't connect to data. Content was pasted in, so the spreadsheet and the prototype were easy to unsync. Copy changes were manual and repeated. One wording fix meant editing every frame it appeared on. Only the person who wired the file could safely maintain it.

Change was slow, risky and hard to hand over.

Regional Go-To-Market (GTM) teams needed to personalize agentic commerce user flow screens for local pitches, but the process created a heavy design bottleneck:

Design Ticket Dependency:
Regional leads had to submit design tickets and wait days just to get localized variants back.
Repetitive Manual Work:
Creating a single personalized variant required 82 manual instance updates across 6 screens, taking roughly 240 minutes (4 hours) per variant.

Democratize how regional teams personalize agentic user flow screens by removing the design ticket bottleneck entirely.

The goal was to empower regional leads to self-serve – going from sourcing brand assets (via tools like CSS Peeper) to a finished, client-ready flow in the time it takes to grab a coffee.

I designed and rolled out a centralized GTM Figma Template built for instant, self-serve customization:

Single-Sync Architecture:
Replaced 82 manual instance updates with a master component system that syncs brand assets across all 6 user flow screens simultaneously.
Zero-Ticket Workflow:
Built clear onboarding instructions directly into the Figma file alongside a 1-minute walkthrough video, allowing non-designers to generate bespoke flows independently.

My Path
in Payments

A high-visibility print project was stalling due to inadequate freelance output and a budget that was about to be swallowed by global shipping costs.

Take ownership of both the editorial design and the physical supply chain to deliver a luxury, coffee-table-grade book without blowing the operational budget.

I stepped in to lead the design in-house, introducing bold, full-bleed imagery and customer-centric typography — and partnered directly with master printers Gavin Martin. Looking at the unit economics across 300 copies (4,000 internal sheets), I re-engineered the paper spec: dropping the internal stock weight by just 30gsm shaved a full metric ton (1,000 kg) off the total freight weight.

The massive savings on global shipping paid for the high-end finishing specs, turning a logistical constraint into a flagship physical artifact.
